We study what makes software hard to maintain, and how to fix it.

The SQM Research Lab helps practitioners build software that is maintainable, dependable, and energy efficient. We work with the evidence left behind in real code histories, and we build the tools that make that evidence usable.

Directed by Dr. Shaiful Chowdhury, Department of Computer Science, University of Manitoba.

Research

Software rarely fails because it was written badly once. It fails because it is changed for years by people who did not write it. Our work measures how that change accumulates, which parts of a system absorb the cost, and what teams can do about it before the cost becomes unmanageable.

Software maintenance

How source code decays over time, which methods become abnormally bug prone, and which metrics genuinely predict maintenance effort rather than merely correlating with size.

Energy-efficient software

Modelling and measuring where software spends energy, from mobile applications to server workloads, so that developers can make informed trade-offs.

Mining software repositories

Reconstructing accurate code histories from version control, issue trackers, and Stack Overflow, and building the tooling that makes large-scale empirical study possible.

Director

Dr. Shaiful Chowdhury

Director. Assistant Professor, Computer Science

Dr. Shaiful Chowdhury directs the SQM Research Lab and is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Computer Science at the University of Manitoba. He completed postdoctoral research at the University of Calgary and the University of British Columbia, and earned his PhD from the University of Alberta, where his dissertation received the Outstanding PhD Thesis Award. He holds an MSc in Computer Science from the University of Saskatchewan and a BSc in Computer Science from the University of Chittagong, Bangladesh.

His research covers software maintenance, software energy modelling and efficiency, and mining software repositories. He received the ACM SIGSOFT Distinguished Paper Award at ICSE 2021, the Early Achievement Award in PhD (Computing Science) from the University of Alberta, and the Mining Challenge Paper Award at MSR 2015. His work has been covered by Global News Canada, UBC News, and University of Alberta News.
