The Software Quality and Maintenance Research Lab studies how real codebases decay, where software wastes energy, and what the history of a project can tell us that the current version cannot.

ResearchWhat we work on

Software rarely fails because it was written badly once. It fails because it is changed for years by people who did not write it.

We build evidence about how that change accumulates, which parts of a system absorb the cost, and what teams can do before the cost becomes unmanageable. Alongside the empirical work, we release tools so that other researchers can reproduce and extend it.

Software maintenance

How source code decays over time, which methods become abnormally bug prone, and which metrics genuinely predict maintenance effort rather than merely tracking size.

Energy-efficient software

Measuring and modelling where software spends energy, from mobile applications to server workloads, so developers can make informed trade-offs.

Mining software repositories

Reconstructing accurate code histories from version control, issue trackers, and Stack Overflow, and building the tooling that makes large-scale empirical study possible.

Director

Dr. Shaiful Chowdhury

Assistant Professor, Computer Science

Dr. Shaiful Chowdhury directs the SQM Research Lab. He completed postdoctoral research at the University of Calgary and the University of British Columbia, and earned his PhD from the University of Alberta, where his dissertation received the Outstanding PhD Thesis Award. He holds an MSc in Computer Science from the University of Saskatchewan and a BSc in Computer Science from the University of Chittagong, Bangladesh.

His research covers software maintenance, software energy modelling and efficiency, and mining software repositories. He received the ACM SIGSOFT Distinguished Paper Award at ICSE 2021, the Early Achievement Award in PhD (Computing Science) from the University of Alberta, and the Mining Challenge Paper Award at MSR 2015. His work has been covered by Global News Canada, UBC News, and University of Alberta News.
